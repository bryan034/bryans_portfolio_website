<h2 id="overview">Overview</h2>

This Financial Analytics academic project explored customer credit risk using historical lending data. The wider project compared supervised learning approaches to loan-default prediction across multiple loan categories.

My work focused on the path from raw data to a credit scorecard: preparing the dataset, shaping useful features, and constructing the scorecard.

<h2 id="contribution">My contribution & project context</h2>

**My ownership: data preparation, feature development, and scorecard construction.** Within the academic project, I personally carried out:

- Data cleaning, categorisation, and merging.
- Data binning and correlation analysis.
- Fine and coarse classing.
- Feature selection and weight-of-evidence (WoE) binning.
- Building the credit scorecard.

The broader supervised-model comparison is project context. The contribution above identifies the work I personally owned, without claiming responsibility for every part of the team's analysis.

<h2 id="technical">Technical approach</h2>

### Preparing the data

I cleaned, categorised, and merged the lending data to prepare it for analysis. This work connects the original records to the features used in the credit-risk workflow.

### Developing the features

I worked on binning, correlation analysis, fine and coarse classing, feature selection, and WoE binning. These tasks formed the feature-development portion of my contribution.

### Building the scorecard

I built the credit scorecard from the prepared inputs. This makes the project a concrete example of my work across data preparation, feature engineering, and a financial analytics deliverable.

The particular tools, thresholds, bin boundaries, scoring formula, and evaluation results are not claimed here until the original analysis is available for review.
