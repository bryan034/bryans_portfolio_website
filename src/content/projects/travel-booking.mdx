<h2 id="overview">Overview</h2>

TravelLust is a team-built travel-planning platform for organising trips and working with flights, hotels, attractions, and bookings. It was developed for SMU's IS213 Enterprise Solution Development module.

The platform uses separate services for its domains, with Python/Flask backends, REST APIs, Docker, Supabase, and RabbitMQ. The shared frontend uses React and TypeScript. These are platform technologies; my personal ownership is described below. [Project documentation](https://github.com/curryriiiice/IS213-travellust#readme).

<h2 id="contribution">My contribution & project context</h2>

**My ownership: microservice architecture and the attraction workflow.** I worked on the microservice architecture and implemented atomic and composite services for adding, booking, and planning attractions.

- **Architecture:** defining how the system's service components fit together.
- **Atomic services:** implementing the attraction-related operations used as building blocks.
- **Composite services:** coordinating those building blocks into attraction booking and planning workflows.

Flights, hotels, collaboration, notifications, and the shared frontend are part of the wider team's product. Their presence in the platform does not imply that I implemented all of them.

<h2 id="technical">Technical approach</h2>

### Adding an attraction

The attraction service accepts a manually entered attraction or an item from the catalogue. It checks that the trip exists, creates the attraction, and adds its identifier to the trip. The catalogue and attraction data use Supabase. [Attraction service documentation](https://github.com/curryriiiice/IS213-travellust/blob/main/my-monorepo/apps/attractions/README.md).

### Coordinating a booking

The booking composite checks that the payer and ticket holders belong to the trip, retrieves the attraction, and calls the booked-ticket service. It validates returned records before reporting success, and publishes booking outcomes to RabbitMQ. Its documented simulated-failure path helps demonstrate unsuccessful bookings. [Booking service documentation](https://github.com/curryriiiice/IS213-travellust/blob/main/my-monorepo/apps/book-attractions/README.md).

### Planning and a concrete trade-off

The attraction-planning component validates required fields and trip membership before calling the attraction service. After saving, it publishes a Redis event for real-time updates. If event publishing fails, it logs the failure and retains the successful save. This prioritises persisted data over immediate notification delivery, while leaving a possible gap in the real-time update. [Planning implementation](https://github.com/curryriiiice/IS213-travellust/blob/main/my-monorepo/apps/plan_service/apps/plan_service/services/attraction_plan_service.py).

These flows illustrate the distinction between domain operations and coordination across services. Docker and Compose package the platform's service processes for local operation. [Deployment overview](https://github.com/curryriiiice/IS213-travellust#readme).
